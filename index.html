Two clients who took their business back

Proof that what we build stays yours. One was paying vendors who controlled their domain and their reviews. One had been locked out by the people who built everything. Both now hold every login themselves.

Held by contract

A service company

Paying about $6,000 a month across three vendors — website, SEO, CRM — and not in control of any of it.

The website vendor held the hosting logins and controlled the domain. The company owned that domain on paper and could not touch it. That one contract was $2,500 a month on an annual term with no way out until November. The SEO vendor controlled their Google account — including their own customer reviews — and they had no access to that either.

We waited out the contracts, then moved every asset into accounts the company holds the usernames and passwords to. A one-time fee, then a small monthly, because once it is set up correctly it is not much work.

If they want to fire us tomorrow, they can.

Everything is already in their name. That is the whole point.

Held by people

A manufacturer's rep company

A falling-out with the employees who had built everything — and those employees kept it.

They had registered the domain, built the site, run the hosting, run the email server, held the entire back end. The company had access to none of it and the old team would not hand it over.

In about 30 days we recovered the domain, repointed DNS, moved to new hosting, built a new website and migrated email onto new admin accounts — every piece of it registered in the company's name. Billed as a monthly consulting fee over twelve months instead of one large sum, because the first three months carry the weight.

They hold the register now.
